Downy Mildew of Rice

Caused by Sclerophthora species

Disease Information

Scientific Name:
Sclerophthora spp.

Distribution:
Worldwide in rice-growing regions

Primary Host:
Rice (Oryza sativa)

Other Hosts:
Beans, crucifers, cucurbits, maize, etc.

Symptoms

  • Initial infection appears as angular yellow spots on upper leaf surface
  • Spots turn brilliant yellow, then brown with yellow margins
  • Fine, grayish fungal growth on leaf undersides
  • White coating of spores on young shoots, fruits, and seeds
  • Visible downy growth on leaf undersides in early morning

Pathogen Identification

The downy mildew fungus produces round, pale yellow, smooth-walled resting spores (oospores) within infected tissues. These structures are visible under a microscope in decolorized leaf tissues stained with acid fuchsin.

Survival & Spread
  • Thick-walled oospores persist in dead host tissue for years
  • Spread via diseased plant residues, soil, seed grain, wind, water, and equipment
Favorable Conditions
  • Cool moist and warm moist weather
  • Presence of weeds in fields
  • Infected leaves left to rot in field
  • Poor plant aeration

Management Strategies

Preventive Methods
  • Use disease-free, resistant seeds
  • Transplant only healthy seedlings
  • Ensure proper land preparation and drainage
  • Provide adequate plant spacing for aeration
  • Prune new growth to improve air circulation
Cultural Methods
  • Remove infested plants when dry
  • Properly dispose diseased parts by burning/burying
  • Avoid overhead watering
  • Plow-under all plant debris after harvest
  • Practice crop rotation with resistant varieties
Chemical Methods
  • Apply Bordeaux mixture
  • Use appropriate seed treatments
  • Fungicides containing metalaxyl or fosetyl-aluminum
  • Apply during early stages of infection
