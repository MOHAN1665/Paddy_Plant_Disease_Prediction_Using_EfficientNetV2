Bacterial Leaf Streak

Caused by Xanthomonas oryzae pv. oryzicola

Disease Information

Common Name:
Rice bacterial leaf streak; rice black chaff

Scientific Name:
Xanthomonas oryzae pv. oryzicola

Distribution:
Asia, Africa, Australasia - restricted

Primary Host:
Rice (Oryza sativa)

Symptoms

  • Initially, small, dark-green and water-soaked streaks on interveins from tillering to booting stage
  • The progress of the streaks is longitudinal, limited by the veins and soon turn yellow or orange brown
  • All along the streaks bacterial exudates could be observed as tiny yellow or amber colored droplets
  • These streaks may coalesce to form large patches and cover the entire leaf surface
  • Lesions turn brown to greyish white then dry when disease is severe
  • Infection in the florets and seeds results in brown or black discoloration and death of ovary, stamens and endosperm and browning of glumes

Pathogen Identification

  • Rod-shaped bacteria (1.2 x 0.3-0.5 µm)
  • Single, occasionally in pairs but not in chains
  • Gram negative, non-spore-forming, no capsules
  • Single polar flagellum for movement
  • Pale yellow, circular colonies on nutrient agar
  • Grows optimally at 28°C
Favorable Conditions
  • Presence of bacteria on leaves and in water or debris
  • Warm temperatures (25-30°C) with high humidity
  • Early stage of planting from maximum tillering to panicle initiation
  • Wet conditions and poor drainage
  • Spread through rain splash, irrigation water, and contaminated tools

Management Strategies

Preventive Methods
  • Use hot water treated seeds (52-54°C for 30 minutes)
  • Seed treatment with bleaching powder (100g/l)
  • Spray fresh cowdung water extract (20%)
  • Apply neem oil 3% or NSKE 5%
  • Maintain field sanitation and remove infected debris
Cultural Methods
  • Plant resistant varieties (IR 20, TKM 6)
  • Use disease-free seed from upland nurseries
  • Avoid clipping of seedlings during transplanting
  • Maintain proper plant spacing for good air circulation
  • Provide good drainage system especially in seedbeds
  • Destroy ratoons, straws and volunteer seedlings
Chemical Methods
  • Spray streptomycin sulphate + tetracycline combination (300g) + copper oxychloride (1.25 kg/ha)
  • Apply bleaching powder @ 5 kg/ha in irrigation water
  • Foliar spray with copper fungicides alternated with Streptocyclin (250 ppm)
  • Repeat sprays at 15-day intervals if needed
  • Spray lemon grass or mint extract at 20% concentration
