Dead Heart of Rice

Caused by Yellow Stem Borer (Scirpophaga incertulas)

Pest Information

Scientific Name:
Scirpophaga incertulas

Primary Host:
Rice (Oryza sativa)

Other Hosts:
Sugarcane, weeds

Damage Potential:
Up to 50% yield loss

Symptoms & Damage

Dead Heart
  • Brown colored egg mass near leaf tip
  • Larva enters stem and feeds on growing shoot
  • Central shoot dries up (dead heart)
  • Yellow coloration of affected tillers
White Ear
  • In mature plants, ear heads become dried
  • Produces chaffy grains (white ear)
  • Affected parts come out easily when pulled
  • Visible feeding injuries at the base

Pest Identification

Eggs
  • Creamy white, flattened, oval
  • Laid in masses covered with buff-colored hairs
  • Mostly near leaf tips
  • Egg period: 5-8 days
Larva
  • Pale yellow with dark brown head
  • Prothoracic shield present
  • Larval period: 28-30 days
Pupa
  • White silken cocoon
  • Pupates inside rice stem, straw or stubble
  • Pupal period: 8-10 days
Adult Moth
  • Female: Bright yellowish brown with black spot on forewings and yellow anal tuft
  • Male: Smaller, pale yellow forewings without black spot

Management Strategies

Cultural Methods
  • Grow resistant varieties (Ratna, TKM 6, IR 20, IR 40, IR 56, etc.)
  • Remove and destroy stubbles after harvest
  • Clip seedling tips before transplanting
  • Collect and destroy egg masses
  • Avoid close planting and water stagnation
  • Pull out and destroy affected tillers
Biological Methods
  • Apply Bacillus thuringiensis var kurstaki (2.5 kg/ha)
  • Release Trichogramma japonicum egg parasitoids
  • 3 releases at weekly intervals starting 28 DAT
  • Avoid chemical sprays 3 days before/after release
  • Release egg cards during morning hours
Chemical Control
  • ETL: 2 egg masses/m² or 10% dead heart or 2% white ear
  • Spray quinalphos or phosphomidon (1000ml/ha) twice at 7-day intervals
  • Options: Quinalphos 25 EC, Phosphamidon 40 SL, Profenophos 50 EC
  • Delay chemical use when natural enemies are present
